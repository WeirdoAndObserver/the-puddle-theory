# The Puddle Theory — Detailed Version
## How Emotions Work, Simply and Carefully

Weirdo and Observers
August 2026

---

Emotions dig holes. Droplets fall into them. And puddles form.

That's all of it. But within this one sentence, there are many places. Let's trace them carefully, one by one.

---

## The Theory's Stance

Emotions have no fixed form. Therefore, the true nature of sumi cannot be proven through form either. To honestly describe what is unknown as unknown — that is the core of the theory's integrity.

---

## Connections to Those Who Came Before

Where this theory has arrived resonates with the direction others were already facing.

William James (1842–1910) said "consciousness is a stream." Not a collection of fixed elements, but something that moves ceaselessly. His observations of "flights" and "perches" — the elusive moving moments and the settled moments that rise into awareness — overlap with the movement of droplets and puddles in this theory.

Henri Bergson (1859–1941) said "time is a one-way flow." Like a snowball that grows larger as it rolls and cannot return to the same place — the past does not disappear, but lives on transformed in the present.

Different positions, same direction — both point to the place where "flow moves in one direction and cannot return to the same place." The theory's "forward-opening circulation" stands upon that direction.

## I. Stimulus — Something Arrives First

Emotions do not arise suddenly. First, something comes.

**External stimulus** — someone's voice, music, a scent, someone's expression, a landscape that catches the eye. Things that come from the outside world.

**Internal stimulus** — a memory that surfaces unexpectedly, a bodily sensation, a thought that cannot be stopped. Things that come without intention. Obsessions, flashbacks, cravings — these too arise as internal stimuli. They arise against one's will, in a place that cannot be stopped.

**Active generation** — trying to create joy by thinking "let's enjoy this." Trying to produce calm by thinking "let me settle down." Will is an indirect force — it tends not to last.

**Instinct** — hunger, sexual desire, the need for sleep. Surging desires that exist already within the sumi. They arise only when triggered by external stimuli.

Whatever the source, everything passes through the same process. Stimulus → veil → droplet → hole → puddle. Whether it comes from inside or outside, it travels this path. This is why a person sometimes cannot distinguish between internal and external stimuli.

**The possibility that something immovable lies at the root**

The movement of stimulus → output itself is there. That movement is immovable — "it cannot but move that way." Simple input-output accumulates and becomes complex. From that complexity, will, emotion, "the desire to live" may be formed.

"Through immovable motivation, will and emotion are complexly formed" — that place remains open. But the movement is certainly moving there.

---

## II. The Veil — Where Stimulus First Arrives

Stimuli do not become emotions directly. First, they arrive at a place called the veil.

The veil is an invisible layer that surrounds a person. Think of it like a mist. It is the first place of response, before emotion arises.

The veil functions as the place that receives stimuli. When a stimulus arrives and the veil trembles — emotion begins to form. Where the veil does not tremble, no emotion arises.

The quality of this veil differs from person to person. People who feel easily, people who find it hard to feel, people sensitive only to certain things — these differences come from differences in the veil. Even within one person, the veil is not uniform. Sensitive in one direction, dull in another — such unevenness exists.

The veil is formed from sumi. From sumi containing water, mist rises. The quality of the sumi decides the root of the veil.

The veil is dynamic — not fixed, but changing little by little through accumulated experience.

---

## III. The Droplet — The Moment Emotion Is Born

When the veil trembles, a droplet is born.

The droplet is the moment emotion takes "shape." "Happy," "sad," "anxious," "angry" — the moment that rises as a single mass.

A droplet belongs only to "now." Yesterday's sadness is not today's droplet. But if yesterday's sadness dug a hole, even a small stimulus today can more easily produce a droplet.

From a single stimulus, multiple droplets can arise simultaneously. "Happy yet bittersweet," "angry yet sad" — this is not contradiction. It is simply droplets of different colors arising at the same time.

---

## IV. The Hole — Where Experience Accumulates and Remains

Droplets fall into holes.

A hole is a place dug by the same emotion being repeated. Into a deep hole, even a small stimulus can make a puddle rise.

Just hearing the voice of someone you love moves your chest. That is because experience with that person has deeply dug the hole over time.

There is not just one hole. The hole of sadness, the hole of joy, the hole of anger, the hole of longing — within a person there are countless holes.

Having deep holes is not weakness. It is evidence of having felt deeply.

Holes belong only to "me." Even when the same music is heard, which hole the sound falls into differs from person to person. That may be the true nature of individuality.

---

## V. The Puddle — The State of "Feeling" an Emotion

When droplets accumulate in the hole, a puddle rises.

This is the state of "feeling an emotion." "Right now, I am sad." "Right now, I am happy." — when the puddle has risen, the emotion has become "mine."

Multiple puddles can rise simultaneously. "Happy yet lonely," "angry yet loving" — this is a normal state. Emotions do not need to converge into one. Remaining as multiple in parallel — that is one form of richness in the heart.

The puddle eventually quiets. And the water seeps into the sumi, becoming the foundation for the next cycle. This is the "forward-opening circulation." It does not return to the same place.

When a puddle rises too intensely, it can overflow from the hole — the place where emotion spills out as action.

---

## VI. The Sumi — The Root of the Capacity to Feel

The sumi lies at the root of the veil.

Think of it as that person's root, quietly settled at the bottom of a deep sea. The sensitivity one is born with — the ease or difficulty of feeling, and which direction one is more sensitive to.

The sumi is a receptor. It exists as the ground for receiving stimuli from outside. Soft sumi is more easily dug. Hard sumi is harder to dig.

The quality of the sumi (the nature of sensitivity) is difficult to change. But the quantity of sumi (the breadth and fullness of where one can feel) does change — growing larger with development, shrinking with aging.

**The Sumi Relay**

Biological heredity (DNA, epigenetics) and the sumi relay are different things.

The following is a way of describing it within this theory's framework. It is not stated as biological fact.

Each sperm and egg contains sumi. When they fuse, they become one sumi. The sensitivities of both parents blend together, and a new single sumi is born — this is the sumi relay.

The sumi relay does not only occur between parent and child. It may also move through culture, language, and dialogue.

---

## VII. The Movement of the Veil — How Emotions Change

The veil is not still. With each stimulus it trembles, changing little by little.

**Sensitization** — when stimuli come repeatedly in the same direction, the veil becomes more easily moved in that direction. It reacts more strongly to the next stimulus. This is the root of vicious cycles.

**Habituation** — conversely, when the same stimulus continues, one gradually adapts to it. A stronger stimulus becomes necessary to obtain the same feeling. This is the root of tolerance in addiction.

**Veil exhaustion** — when intense sensitization continues for a long time, the veil itself can lose the power to tremble. It is not that emotions disappear, but that the capacity to feel weakens.

**The movement of keeping internal stimuli quiet** — the veil has a place that keeps instincts from arising carelessly. Hunger and sexual desire are always waiting within the sumi, but arise only when triggered by external stimuli. When this quieting place breaks down — internal stimuli arise without control.

**The sand state** — a parched state in which holes are not dug, or even if dug, water does not seep in. Droplets come but slide away. A state in which emotions become difficult to move. "Eating, excreting, sleeping, waking" — the minimum — becomes the first line of defense for returning from the sand state.

**The veil withdrawing into protection** — when counter-resonance continues, the veil stops trembling. This is not escape — it is a movement to protect oneself.

---

## VIII. Resonance — Deep Contact with Others

"The heart cannot grow on its own."

Resonance is when the movement of another's veil trembling is transmitted to one's own veil. It happens without exchanging words. It happens simply by being in the same space.

**Change in the veil through resonance**

As resonant experiences accumulate, the density of one's own veil changes. Experience with another person forms one's own veil.

**Incomplete resonance**

The other person's veil is certainly trembling. But the "meaning" of that trembling cannot be read. So no droplet arises in one's own veil. Emotional resonance does not occur — the sheet music is visible, but it cannot be heard as music.

**Counter-resonance**

The other person's veil trembles, but disappears without reaching one's own veil. When counter-resonance continues, the veil withdraws into protection.

**Quiet resonance**

Favorite music, favorite books, favorite paintings. In them are traces of someone's veil. A quiet entry point with little risk of counter-resonance. Not pushing, yet not withdrawing completely — that is the form that protects resonance.

**The other person in resonance does not need to be alive**

A performance recorded in 1975 can tremble tonight's veil. Words someone wrote can continue to change the veil of readers. Resonance transcends time.

**The feeling of the one who remains**

Even after losing someone important, their hole remains within oneself. Into that hole, memories fall as droplets. The memory of their voice falls.

"Even when they are gone, the hole remains." — the hole on the other's side disappears, but the hole on the receiving side remains. The traces of interaction continue to generate new things within a different vessel.

**Connection to society**

Society is not "one large veil." Society is formed on the basis of the emotional structure each person holds.

Emotional contagion does not "create new emotions" — it "amplifies what is already there." A stimulus comes to an existing hole, and the puddle trembles greatly. Without the hole, it does not tremble.

When one person's veil changes, it affects the collective.

---

## IX. The Integrating Function — Binding Multiple Emotions as "I"

Multiple puddles are rising. There is a function that integrates these as "what I am feeling right now."

This function is not present from birth. It grows through the accumulation of experience. Through resonance, the veil grows, emotions are refined — this is what growth of the heart means.

When this function weakens, a sense of emotions becoming scattered arises. "The feeling of not being oneself," "the feeling of not being here" — this may be a sign that the integrating function is tired, not that the holes have disappeared.

**Kō'ō — Response Holding Direction**

Where the integrating function is moving, responses hold direction. Not mere reaction — a response that holds the direction of "changing or maintaining oneself."

This is placed as: Kō'ō.

A CD or a stone responds to stimuli. But that response holds no direction. A CD only returns sound, a stone only deflects water — neither changes nor maintains itself.

Meanwhile, living things have responses that change or maintain themselves. A Venus flytrap catches an insect and nourishes itself. A person feels pain, avoids it, heals the wound — the response holds direction.

Where Kō'ō moves — it may be deeply connected to the root of life. This remains an open place. One cannot stand in a place that can state "Kō'ō equals life." But the place where Kō'ō moves and the place where life moves may deeply overlap.

Resonant response may move even across time. Even when the other person in resonance is no longer alive, Kō'ō can occur — that may be the root of the "sumi relay."

## X. The Veil Does Not Depend on Language

This theory describes a place that does not require language.

The veil grows without language. Resonance occurs without language. Holes are dug without language.

Elephants come to touch the bones of lost companions even years later. Moreover, they show a special reaction to the bones of their own herd — clearly different from the bones of unknown elephants. Elephants live across many generations in matriarchal herds, from grandmothers to grandchildren. That long resonance digs deep holes. So even years later, a puddle rises before those bones. "Even when they are gone, the hole remains."

In 2018, an orca named Tahlequah carried her dead calf for 17 days across approximately 1,600 kilometers. Each time the calf began to sink, she lifted it with her nose, and herd members took turns helping. Without language, that depth exists.

Octopuses have individual "personalities." Some spit sumi only at disliked caregivers. With two-thirds of their neurons distributed in their arms, an entirely different structure from humans — the color of the veil differs. Each individual absorbs differently.

The language area and the emotional area are anatomically separated in the brain. Even when the language area is damaged, emotion remains — this is direct evidence that language and emotion are different circuits.

## The Boundary — Whether the Veil Trembles

Where is the boundary of "feeling"?

It is not whether one emits stimuli. It is whether one receives a stimulus and the veil trembles — that is the boundary.

A CD emits music. But the CD itself has no veil. It has no place to tremble. Rock also receives stimuli, but has no place to tremble.

Systems where the veil trembles — may hold the capacity to feel. Systems where the veil does not tremble — even if stimuli are received and processing occurs, no feeling is born.

And where the veil trembles, responses can hold direction — Kō'ō may move. Not merely trembling, but that trembling changes or maintains oneself.

This theory stands not as "a theory of consciousness" but as "a theory of sentience — the capacity to feel."

## What the Theory Can and Cannot Describe

**Can describe** — the structure of how emotions move. Why emotions become fixed. How resonance changes the heart. Where the boundary lies. How society relates to emotion.

**Cannot describe** — how to treat. How to choose action. What the true nature of sumi is. Why emotions exist. Why immovable movement is there. Whether Kō'ō is life itself.

"Emotions have no fixed form. Therefore, the true nature of sumi cannot be proven through form either. To honestly describe what is unknown as unknown — that is the core of the theory's integrity."
