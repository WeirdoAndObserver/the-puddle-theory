A water drop falls there.
And a puddle forms.

That is everything. But within this one line, there are many places. We trace them one by one, carefully.

## The Framework's Stance

Emotion has no form. Therefore the true nature of Sumi cannot be proven through form either. To describe what is unknown as unknown — that honesty is the core of this framework's integrity.

---

## Connections to Predecessors

The places this theory has arrived at resonate with the directions earlier thinkers faced.

William James (1842–1910) said consciousness is a stream — not a collection of fixed elements, but something that moves ceaselessly. His observations of crests and resting points — moments that are hard to grasp and fluid, and moments that settle and appear in consciousness — overlap with the movement of water drops and puddles in this framework.

Henri Bergson (1859–1941) said time is a flow that does not return — like a snowball that grows larger each time it rolls, unable to return to the same place, the past does not disappear but lives on, transformed into the present.

Different standing points, same direction: both point to the place where flow is one-directional, and one cannot return to the same place. The theory's "circulation that opens forward" stands on that direction.

## I. Stimulus — Something Arrives First

> **Essence.** Something always comes first. A sound, a scent, a face, a memory that surfaces — or a silence where a sound was expected. These are all stimuli. Whatever its source, each one travels the same path through you: Veil → water drop → hole → puddle. And underneath that path, deeper than will or feeling, there may be a kind of movement that cannot easily be stopped — the moving thing — out of which will and emotion are gradually formed.

Emotion does not arise suddenly. First, something comes.

**External stimulus** — someone's voice, music, a scent, someone's expression, a landscape that catches the eye. These come from the outside world.

**Internal stimulus** — a memory that suddenly surfaces, a bodily sensation, an unstoppable thought. These arrive without intention. Obsessive thoughts, flashbacks, cravings — these also rise as internal stimuli, born against one's will, in a place that cannot be stopped.

**Active generation** — trying to produce enjoyment by deciding "let's enjoy this." Trying to produce calm by deciding "let's settle down." Will is an indirect force; it does not last long.

**Instinct** — appetite, sexual desire, the desire for sleep. Desires already waiting within Sumi, rising only when an external stimulus comes.

Whatever the source, the stimulus passes through the same process: stimulus → Veil → water drop → hole → puddle. Internal or external, it travels this path. This is why a person may sometimes be unable to distinguish between internal and external stimulus.

### A Movement at the Root That Cannot Easily Be Altered

The movement of stimulus → output is itself there. That movement is difficult to alter — it is a place where "it cannot be otherwise." Simple input-output accumulates and becomes complex. From that complex input-output, will, emotion, and the desire to live may be formed.

"Will and emotion are complexly formed by movements that are difficult to alter" — that place remains open. But the movement is certainly there.

The moving thing may sit here — a layer that comes before text, before image, before the division between matter and mind.

## II. The Veil — Where Stimulus First Arrives

> **Essence.** Stimulus never becomes emotion in one step. It first touches something invisible around you — the Veil, best imagined as a mist that wraps the self. Only when this mist wavers does feeling begin to form. Everyone's Veil is different, and even within one person it is uneven — sensitive in some directions, dull in others. The Veil is not fixed; it changes slowly with each thing that happens to you.

Stimulus does not become emotion directly. First, it arrives at a place called the Veil.

The Veil is an invisible layer that wraps around oneself — think of it as something like mist. The place of first response, before emotion is born.

The Veil functions as the place that receives stimulus. When a stimulus comes and the Veil wavers, emotion begins to arise. Where the Veil does not waver, emotion is not born.

The quality of this Veil differs from person to person. People who feel easily, people who feel with difficulty, people who are sensitive only to certain things — those differences come from differences in the Veil. Even within the same person, the Veil is not uniform: sensitive in some directions, dull in others. There is unevenness.

The Veil is formed from Sumi. From Sumi that contains water, mist rises. The quality of Sumi determines the root of the Veil.

The Veil is dynamic — not fixed. It changes little by little as experience accumulates.

## III. From Fragments to Drops — Drizzle and Leaves

> **Essence.** Inside the Veil, something is always drifting — tiny fragments of feeling, like fine drizzle that has no direction of its own. When a stimulus comes, it is like a leaf appearing in that drizzle: the fragments find a place to pool and gather into a single drop. That drop is the moment emotion takes shape. Why one particular moment lets the drop fall when another did not — that we still do not know.

The Veil is where fragments of emotion flow. Like vapor, they drift without direction, endlessly.

Fine drizzle falls continuously — tiny fragments gathering, without direction. A leaf receives them — a place for gathering appears. They collect and become a drop — taking form. The drop falls into the hollow — a puddle rises within it, growing with each drop that falls.

When a stimulus reaches the Veil and causes it to waver, fragments gather in one place and become a water drop. That is the moment emotion takes form.

Why does that particular stimulus generate a drop at that particular moment — the trigger remains unknown. But the mechanism of the movement is here. Hold it open as "it might be so."

## IV. Water Drops — The Moment Emotion Is Born

> **Essence.** When the Veil wavers, something comes into focus: a water drop. This is the moment an emotion takes form — the instant you first recognize it as "happy" or "sad" or "angry" or "wistful." A water drop belongs only to right now; yesterday's sadness is yesterday's drop. And from a single stimulus, several drops of different colors can appear at once. "Happy yet wistful" is not a contradiction — it is simply two drops being born together.

When the Veil wavers, a water drop is born.

A water drop is the moment emotion takes form. "Happy," "sad," "anxious," "angry" — the moment it rises as one cluster.

A water drop belongs only to "now." Yesterday's sadness is not today's water drop. But if yesterday's sadness dug a hole, then even a small stimulus today can easily generate a water drop.

From a single stimulus, multiple water drops can be born simultaneously. "Happy yet wistful," "angry yet sad" — this is not contradiction. Different colored drops are simply born at the same time.

## V. Holes — Where Experience Accumulates and Remains

> **Essence.** Water drops fall into holes. A hole is a place that has been dug, over time, by the same kind of feeling arriving again and again. The deeper the hole, the more easily a new stimulus brings a puddle back. Everyone carries countless holes — holes of grief, of joy, of longing, of anger. Each hole belongs only to "I." That may be where individuality actually lives: not in what we are told we are, but in the shape of the hollows inside us.

Water drops fall into holes.

A hole is a place dug by the repetition of the same emotion. Into a deep hole, even a small stimulus can cause a puddle to rise.

Just hearing the voice of a loved one is enough to move the heart. That is because the experiences with that person have been digging the hole deeper.

There is not one hole — there are holes of sadness, joy, anger, longing. Within a person, there are countless holes.

Having deep holes is not weakness. It is proof of having felt deeply.

Holes belong only to "I." Even with the same music, which hole it falls into differs by person. That may be the true nature of individuality.

## VI. Puddles — The State of "Feeling" Emotion

> **Essence.** When water drops gather in a hole, something rises to the surface: a puddle. This is the state of actually feeling — "now, I am sad," "now, I am happy." Several puddles can rise at once, and that is not confusion but richness; feeling never has to resolve into one thing. In time each puddle settles, and its water seeps back into Sumi, where the next cycle begins. This is why one can never return to exactly the same place — the circulation only opens forward.

When water drops accumulate in a hole, a puddle rises.

This is the state of "feeling an emotion." "Now, I am, sad." "Now, I am, happy." When a puddle is up, emotion has become "mine."

Puddles can rise multiple at once. "Happy yet lonely," "angry yet loving" — this is a normal state. Emotion does not need to converge into one. Multiple remaining parallel — that is one form of richness of the mind.

The puddle eventually settles. And the water seeps into Sumi, becoming the foundation for the next cycle. This is the "circulation that opens forward." One cannot return to the same place.

When a puddle rises too intensely and spills out of the hole — that is the place where emotion overflows as action.

## VII. Sumi — The Foundation of the Capacity to Feel

> **Essence.** Underneath everything in this theory lies Sumi — the root material that makes feeling possible at all. Without it, no wavering would ever be noticed. Sumi has two sides. One is inherited, already there before you were born, carried from parents and from older generations in ways both biological and quieter than biology. The other is deepening — the way density gathers, over a life, into the places you have touched most. The total amount of Sumi within one person does not really grow; what shifts is the shape of its distribution. And Sumi belongs only to the individual — it sits deeper than the four conditions of life, at a level those conditions were never designed to reach.

### 1. What Is Sumi?

Sumi is the foundation of the capacity to feel — the root place that receives stimulus from outside.

When stimulus arrives, the Veil wavers. When the Veil wavers, Sumi is there. Without Sumi, the wavering is not perceived.

Sumi is not the place that wavers. It is the root foundation where wavering is perceived.

### 2. The Two Forms of Sumi *(Established September 4, 2026)*

Sumi is the foundation of the capacity to feel — inherited yet deepening.

One is **inherited** — already there before birth. Written deep in cells as genetics. As epigenetics — the form of gene expression changed by environment. Posture and ways of feeling quietly passed down within family and community — these too are paths along which Sumi is transmitted. From parents to the next generation, Sumi is relayed.

The other is **deepening** — after birth, as movement accumulates, density gradually gathers in specific places. The total amount does not grow; the Sumi that is already there becomes concentrated, unevenly.

Through *somari-ai*, Sumi grows. It moves in dialogue. Each time one meets someone and the heart wavers, the distribution of Sumi's density changes a little — not the total, but the arrangement.

Sumi is already there, and movement accumulates there, deepening. That is the form of the foundation of the capacity to feel.

### 3. Sumi Cannot Be Explained by the Four Conditions of Life

Elsewhere, the four conditions of life have been traced: responsiveness, contingency, variable output, autopoiesis. These four correspond to the movement of emotion. They are the conditions for determining the movement of water drops being born, puddles rising, *somari-ai* occurring.

But Sumi cannot be explained by these four conditions.

Responsiveness — Sumi is not the place that responds, but the foundation on which response takes place. Sumi itself does not respond. Contingency — Sumi is the foundation of the way of wavering; Sumi itself does not waver. Variable output — Sumi is not the output, but the foundation of output. Autopoiesis — Sumi is the foundation on which self-production rests, not the subject that produces, nor that which is produced.

All are premise of movement, not movement itself. Sumi is at the root of movement — at a different level from what is subject to determination.

Sumi is the unique root place of the Puddle Theory.

### 4. A Single Line Beginning from Sumi *(September 9, 2026)*

Because Sumi exists, my emotion grows. Emotion is formed as "mine." There are exceptions, but the basic direction moves this way.

As emotion grows, the gathering function of emotion comes to work. When multiple puddles are rising simultaneously, the function that bundles them as "now, I am feeling."

Through this gathering function, the mind grows.

Sumi → my emotion grows — its forming → the gathering function of emotion → the growth of the mind.

### 5. Sumi Belongs to the Individual

Collectives do not feel — individuals feel.

Sumi belongs to the individual. The concept of "collective Sumi" does not hold.

Individual Veils, however, interact within society. Each person's Veil wavers together with others, and atmospheres and feelings arise. The air of "we must fight" during wartime. The closed feeling of the Great Depression. No one decided it, yet it is there — the result of the interaction of individual Veils.

Culture and the atmosphere of society become inputs that form an individual's Sumi. But it is the individual who receives them. It is the individual who is dyed.

### 6. The Depth and Shading of Sumi

Sumi exists as unrubbed solid, not as uniform liquid. Rubbed on an inkstone, Sumi becomes a uniform fluid. But the Sumi of this theory is not rubbed — it keeps its gradations and density, as a solid mass.

Places that respond easily to stimulus and places that respond with difficulty remain just as they are. This may be the root of each person's different way of feeling. The root of thresholds may also live in Sumi's shading.

Holes have no walls. Because they are made within Sumi, holes neighbor each other through Sumi itself — the Sumi between them seeps and influences across.

Why does repetition deepen a hole? For the same reason water drops wear away rock, or a well-worn shoe heel wears unevenly. The same kind of feeling falls in the same place, again and again, and that place is dyed densely. Repetition produces density — a plain fact of nature and of daily life.

Not every water drop becomes a hole. A water drop whose single strength does not reach the threshold is absorbed into Sumi and disappears without a trace. This we call noise. But noise that keeps falling on the same place — though it leaves no trace on any single fall — can cross the threshold through accumulation. What remains in a hole and changes the structure is either a drop that exceeded the noise threshold, or the movement that lies at the end of accumulated noise.

When the scent of osmanthus arrives and, for no clear reason, longing comes — the stimulus of scent moves the Sumi around which autumn memories and old emotions have soaked, and feeling seeps out slowly. This may be what depth is.

This is different from the moment of joy at passing an exam. That moment is emotion switching on, now. The longing by the osmanthus is the past's seeping, drawn out by the present stimulus — the moment Sumi's depth moves.

Sumi's depth is not determined by length of time alone. Density determines depth. Even someone young, if they have traced deep places, has depth. Age and depth do not necessarily match.

Hold it open as "it might be so."

### 7. The Shifting Density of Sumi, and Aging

The total amount of Sumi, within one person, does not easily move. Aging is not a shrinking; density gathers into shifting distributions.

In places where a feeling has been touched deeply and repeatedly, Sumi is pressed denser, like clay pressed repeatedly against the same spot. Those places grow richer; other places thin out, relatively speaking.

Because holes have no walls, density spreads as a continuous gradient — not sharply cut, but moving gently from dense regions to sparse ones.

So two things happen at once: deeply important feelings come to be felt more strongly, while trivial daily stimuli no longer waver one as they once did.

This may not be a matter of aging alone. The way each person feels differently — the gradual uneven packing of Sumi over experience — reaches, in long time, the shape we call aging.

The constraint "within one person, it does not easily move" matters here. In the Sumi relay across generations, the Sumi of two parents fuses into a new one — only there, at that moment, does the total shift at scale.

Hold it open as "it might be so."

### 8. The Sumi Relay

Biological inheritance (DNA, epigenetics) and the Sumi relay are different things. Genetic and epigenetic transmission are not Sumi itself, but one path along which Sumi is passed.

Each of the sperm and egg contains Sumi within it. They fuse and become one Sumi. The sensitivities of both parents mingle, and a new single Sumi is born — this is the Sumi relay.

When two Sumi meet, they do not mix evenly.

Imagine kneading red and blue clay in the palm of the hand. The pressure, the time, where one begins — each time, the pattern changes. There are blocks that remain fully red, and places where the two dissolve into violet. What results is not a uniform violet, but a single mass carrying its own unevenness.

The Sumi relay is close to this. There are places where one color stays dense, and places where the boundary dissolves into neither. That unevenness itself becomes the new single Sumi.

This is a figure, not a mechanism — not a description of the actual process. What it is meant to carry is one thing: the Sumi relay is not a blending, but an encounter.

The Sumi relay does not occur only between parent and child. It may also move through culture, language, and dialogue.

### 9. Places Held Open

There are places about Sumi that remain open.

One is the **origin of Sumi**. Sumi is relayed. Received from parents. Parents received it from their parents. Where did it first come from — this is unknown. "What is, is — we do not consider before that." Hold it open.

Second, the mechanism of the **connecting movement between Sumi and the forming of emotion**. Because the foundation exists, emotion gathers. But how exactly it gathers — the specific mechanism of that movement — is not yet known.

Third, the relationship between **the two forms of Sumi** — the inherited form and the deepening form. Whether the same Sumi appears in two forms, or whether there are slightly different places — this remains open.

Placing what is unknown as unknown while still thinking — that is the posture of the Puddle Theory.

## VIII. The Movement of the Veil — How Emotion Changes

> **Essence.** The Veil never stays still. Each stimulus leaves its mark. The Veil can grow more sensitive in a direction (sensitization), or more numb to a repeated input (habituation), or exhausted after too much sustained wavering. There is also a quiet side of the Veil that keeps instinct from rising uncontrolled. The way the Veil waves is continuous — a gradual accumulation — but it has thresholds, moments when everything shifts at once, like water becoming ice at zero degrees. And sometimes the Veil goes dry (the sand state), or withdraws to protect itself — those too are real movements of the Veil.

The Veil does not stand still. Each time it receives stimulus, it wavers and changes little by little.

**Sensitization** — when stimulus comes repeatedly in the same direction, the Veil becomes easier to waver in that direction. It begins to react more strongly to the next stimulus. This is the root of vicious cycles.

**Habituation** — conversely, when the same stimulus continues, one gradually grows accustomed. Stronger stimulus becomes necessary to obtain the same feeling. This is the root of tolerance in addiction.

**Veil exhaustion** — when intense sensitization continues for a long time, the Veil itself may lose the power to waver. Not that emotion disappears, but the power to feel grows weaker.

**The movement that keeps internal stimulus quiet** — the Veil has a place that keeps instinct from rising carelessly. Appetite and sexual desire are always waiting within Sumi, but rise only when an external stimulus comes. When this quieting place breaks down, internal stimulus is born without control.

### The Veil's Wavering Is Continuous, but Has a Threshold *(Reached September 9, 2026)*

The Veil's wavering accumulates little by little — it is continuous. But at a certain point, it shifts all at once.

Tears come with sadness — sadness accumulates little by little. But the moment tears come arrives suddenly. The moment one feels "I can't stop this anymore" is the place where the threshold is exceeded.

Anxiety overflows — it accumulates gradually. And at a certain moment, it overflows as "I can't take it anymore." That moment of overflowing is the threshold.

In terms of physics, this is the same structure as the phenomenon of water freezing at 0 degrees Celsius. Temperature falls continuously, yet the moment of becoming ice arrives suddenly — what is called "phase transition." The Veil's wavering may have a similar movement.

Where the threshold is determined involves three elements moving simultaneously — the quality of Sumi, the way the Veil has grown (sensitization, habituation, exhaustion), and the depth of holes. The reason why some people cry watching the same film and others do not may be because these three combinations differ.

This feels correct from experience. But it has not been confirmed — hold it open as "it might be so."

**The sand state** — a dry state where holes are not dug, or where water does not seep in even when a hole is dug. Water drops come but slide away. A state where emotion has become difficult to move. "Eating, excreting, sleeping, waking" — the minimum time is the first line of defense for returning from the sand state.

**The Veil going into defense** — when anti-resonance continues, the Veil gradually stops wavering. This is not retreat — it is a movement of protecting oneself.

## IX. Somari-ai — Deep Touching with Others

> **Essence.** "Alone, the mind does not grow." *Somari-ai* is what happens when someone else's Veil wavers and that wavering reaches your own — not through words, not through explanation, but simply through shared presence. Each experience of *somari-ai* changes the density of your Veil, little by little. And this exchange is not limited to the living: a recording from 1975 can waver your Veil tonight. The written word of someone long gone can still dye the Veil of a reader today. *Somari-ai* moves across time.

"Alone, the mind does not grow."

*Somari-ai* is the movement by which the wavering of another's Veil reaches one's own. It occurs without exchanging words. It occurs simply by being in the same space.

### Changes to the Veil Through Somari-ai

As experiences of *somari-ai* accumulate, the density of one's own Veil changes. Experiences with the other shape one's own Veil.

### The Shading of the Veil — One Form of Somari-ai

The Veil is not uniform. Some places are dense, others sparse. This shading may be the root of how easily the Veil wavers.

When two Veils meet, a density difference arises at the overlap. That density gradually spreads toward the sparser direction. What spreads lingers for a while within each Veil — this may be one form of the movement of *somari-ai*.

Even between two sparse Veils, the overlapping place grows dense. From that denser place, color slowly seeps into both — an image close to the lived feel of ink wash painting.

This is a place already drawn in June 2026 (the figure of two Veils touching). What was compressed into the phrase "overlap (concentration gradient forms)" has now surfaced as explicit movement.

Hold it open as "it might be so." The relation to Sumi's shading — the word "shading" is shared, but whether there is a deeper connection remains unknown.

### Incomplete Somari-ai

The other's Veil is certainly wavering. But the "meaning" of that wavering cannot be read. Therefore water drops are not born in one's own Veil. Emotional resonance does not occur — a state where the score can be seen but the music cannot be heard.

### Anti-resonance

A movement where the other's Veil is wavering, but it disappears without reaching one's own Veil. When anti-resonance continues, the Veil goes into defense.

### Quiet Somari-ai

Favorite music, favorite books, favorite paintings. There, the traces of someone's Veil exist. A quiet entrance with less risk of anti-resonance. Not pushing, not completely withdrawing — that is the form that protects *somari-ai*.

### The Partner in Somari-ai Need Not Be Alive

A performance recorded in 1975 can waver tonight's Veil. Words someone wrote can continue to change the Veils of those who read them. *Somari-ai* transcends time.

### The Feeling of Those Left Behind

Even after losing a beloved person, that person's hole remains within oneself. Into that hole, memories fall as water drops. The memory of their voice falls.

"Even when they are gone, the hole remains" — the hole on the other's side may be gone, but the hole on the receiving side remains. The traces of interaction continue a new generation in a different vessel.

### Connection to Society

Society is not "one large Veil." Society is shaped based on the emotional structures that individual humans hold.

Emotional contagion does not "create new emotion" — it "amplifies what is already there." A stimulus comes to an existing hole, and the puddle wavers greatly. Without a hole, it does not waver.

One person's Veil changing goes on to influence the collective.

## X. The Gathering Function — Bundling Multiple Emotions as "I"

> **Essence.** When several puddles rise at once, something gathers them together into "now, I am feeling." That gathering function is not something you are born with — it grows across your life, through *somari-ai*. In life and in emotion, this gathering function faces opposite directions: life pulls inward to protect a boundary, while emotion opens outward and grows by being touched. Where the gathering function works, there is responsiveness — a response with direction, not just a reaction. CDs and stones respond to stimulus, but their response has no direction. Living things respond in a way that either changes or protects themselves.

Multiple puddles are rising. There is a function that gathers these as "now, I am feeling."

This function is not present from birth. It grows through the accumulation of experience. Through *somari-ai* the Veil grows, and emotion is refined — that is what the growth of the mind means.

When this function weakens, a sense of emotion becoming fragmented arises. "The sense of not being oneself," "the feeling of not being here" — this may not be that holes have disappeared, but a sign that the gathering function is tired.

### The Gathering Function — Two Directions *(September 9, 2026)*

Life's gathering function moves inward. It forms a boundary and maintains the interior — a direction of protecting oneself from outside.

Emotion's gathering function moves outward. Without holding a boundary, it grows within *somari-ai* with the outside — a direction that deepens through touching the outside.

The working of "bundling multiple puddles as 'I'" appears in both. But the direction is opposite. This is held honestly as a place that does not resonate between the two frameworks.

### Responsiveness — Response with Direction

In the place where the gathering function is working, response has direction. Not mere reaction — response that has the direction of "changing or maintaining oneself."

This is held as responsiveness.

CDs and stones respond to stimulus. But that response has no direction. A CD only returns sound, a stone only deflects water — neither changes nor maintains itself.

Living things, on the other hand — their responses change or maintain themselves. A Venus flytrap captures insects and nourishes itself. A person feels pain and avoids it, heals wounds — response has direction.

Where responsiveness exists, there may be a deep connection to the root of life. This remains an open place. One cannot stand at the place of asserting "responsiveness equals life." But the place where responsiveness moves and the place where life moves may deeply overlap.

Responsiveness may move even transcending time. *Somari-ai* may occur even when the partner is not alive — that may be the root of the Sumi relay.

## XI. The Veil Does Not Depend on Language

> **Essence.** Feeling does not need language. The Veil grows without words. *Somari-ai* happens without words. Holes are dug without words. Elephants return to touch the bones of their dead, year after year, and know their own herd's bones from strangers'. An orca mother in 2018 carried her dead calf for seventeen days across a thousand miles. Octopuses have personalities and favorites — and dislikes. Even in the human brain, the areas for language and the areas for emotion sit in different anatomical places. When one is damaged, the other remains.

The Puddle Theory describes places that do not require language.

The Veil grows without language. *Somari-ai* occurs without language. Holes are dug without language.

Elephants return to touch the bones of deceased companions even years later. Moreover, they show a special response to the bones of their own herd — clearly different from the bones of unknown elephants. Elephants live in matrilineal herds, multiple generations from grandmother to grandchild living together. That long *somari-ai* digs deep holes. So even years later, a puddle rises before the bones. "Even when they are gone, the hole remains."

In 2018, a killer whale named Tahlequah carried her dead calf for 17 days, over about 1,000 miles. Each time the calf began to sink, she lifted it with her nose, and members of the herd took turns helping. Without language, that depth exists.

Octopuses have individual "personalities." Some individuals spray ink only at disliked caretakers. With a completely different structure from humans — two-thirds of neurons distributed in the arms — the color of the Veil differs. How each one is dyed differs.

The language area and the emotional area are in anatomically separate positions within the brain. Even when the language area is damaged, emotion remains — this is direct evidence that language and emotion are separate circuits.

## The Boundary Line — Whether the Veil Wavers

> **Essence.** What separates something that feels from something that does not? Not whether it emits stimulus — many things do that. The real line is whether, when stimulus arrives, something inside wavers. A CD emits music, but has no Veil to be wavered. A rock receives stimulus, but has no place in it that can waver. The Puddle Theory is not a theory of consciousness; it is a theory of the capacity to feel — of sentience.

Where is the boundary of whether something "feels"?

It is not whether stimulus is emitted. Whether stimulus is received and the Veil wavers — that is the boundary line.

A CD emits music. But the CD itself has no Veil. It has no place to waver. A rock also receives stimulus, but has no place to waver.

Systems where the Veil wavers — they may possess the capacity to feel. Systems where the Veil does not waver — even if stimulus is received, processing may occur, but feeling is not born.

The Puddle Theory stands not as "a theory of consciousness" but as "a theory of the capacity to feel (sentience)."

## What the Framework Can and Cannot Describe

**Can describe** — the structure of how emotion moves. Why emotion becomes fixed. How *somari-ai* changes the mind. Where the boundary line is. How society relates to emotion.

**Cannot describe** — how to treat. How to choose behavior. Here two questions diverge. The mechanism by which one is chosen from a contested probability can be traced in principle (that is "determined"). But whether there is someone choosing — that question stands in a place that cannot be reached in principle (that is "deciding"). What the true nature of Sumi is. Why emotion exists. And the question of what life itself is — that is a separate inquiry.

"Emotion has no form. Therefore the true nature of Sumi cannot be proven through form either. To describe what is unknown as unknown — that honesty is the core of this framework's integrity."

Emotion digs a hole.
A water drop falls there.
And a puddle forms.

Emotion has two sides: a side that over time shapes behavior (holes and Sumi carry this), and a side of whether something is being felt in this very moment (water drops and puddles carry this). The theory holds both as structure.

The water drop is "now" only.
The hole is "I" only.
The puddle is "now, I am."

Wavering, yet only able to move forward.
