**A Framework for Emotional Dynamics**

> **Note on terminology:** Three Japanese terms are used throughout this text without translation, as no English equivalent fully captures their meaning. *Sumi* (墨) — the foundational material of emotional capacity, analogous to ink or charcoal. *Somari-ai* (染まり合い) — the mutual dyeing or diffusion of emotional atmosphere between people. *Veil* is the English rendering of the theory's atmospheric concept.

## Prologue — The Core of the Theory

This theory has three central expressions. Each illuminates the same core from a different angle.

**Expression through verbs**
Emotion digs a hole.
A water drop falls there.
And a puddle forms.

**Expression through time and person**
The water drop exists only in "now."
The hole exists only in "I."
The puddle is "now, I am feeling."

**Expression through irreversibility**
Wavering, yet only able to move forward.

The verbal expression shows the structure of the mind's movement. Emotion digs a hole, a drop falls, a puddle forms — these three actions describe the process of the mind's generation and maintenance.

The temporal and personal expression shows the phase for the one who experiences. The drop is the raw emotion that exists only in "now," the hole is the owned emotion that belongs only to "I," the puddle is the present experience of "now, I am feeling."

The expression of irreversibility shows the direction of the mind's movement. Wavering, yet only able to move forward. There is no return.

All chapters that follow are developments of these three cores. When lost in complex discussion, return here.

# Part One — The Flow of Emotion

The movement of the mind can be described as a dynamic process from stimulus to puddle. Presenting this process step by step is the role of Part One.

## Chapter One — Stimulus

The movement of emotion begins with stimulus.

Stimulus refers to any change in energy in general. Sound, light, words, changes in temperature, the movement of someone's expression, events — all of these act on something as changes in energy.

What is important is that stimulus is not only an increase or inflow of energy. The absence of energy — silence, something expected that does not come, the absence of a response from someone — these are also a form of stimulus.

A quiet room with no sound is not simply "nothing." It acts on the listener in the form of "there is no sound." This is a stimulus as the absence of energy.

Stimulus acts first on what is called the "Veil." The Veil is discussed in detail in Part Two. Here, first take note of this two-stage process: stimulus causes the Veil to waver, and from that wavering, emotion is born.

## Chapter Two — Water Drops (Emotion)

When the Veil is caused to waver by a stimulus, a water drop is born.

A water drop is a condensation of emotion. A wavering that, at a certain moment, gathers into a form felt as "this is an emotion" — that is a water drop.

### The Nature of Water Drops — Rain and Hail

Not all water drops have the same nature. Water drops take at least two forms.

**Rain** — water drops that fall as liquid. Gentle, fluid, changing something slowly over time. Most everyday emotions fall as rain. A single drop has no large impact, but repeated drops create change.

**Hail** — water drops that fall as solid. Intense, leaving deep impact in a single fall. Traumatic experiences, sudden losses, shocking events — these fall as hail. Without passing through fluidity, they remain immediately as structure.

### The Quantity and Kind of Water Drops

The factors that determine the nature of water drops can be separated into two.

The **quantity** (number) of water drops is determined by the intensity of the stimulus. A strong stimulus generates many drops, a weak one generates few.

The **kind** (color) of water drops is determined by the state of the Veil at that moment. Even with the same stimulus, different colors of drops are born if the state of the Veil differs. That is why different emotions arise in response to the same event — depending on the person, or even for the same person at different times.

And from a single stimulus, multiple water drops are born simultaneously. Emotions like "happy yet sad" are not contradictions — they are the result of different colored drops being born simultaneously from the same stimulus.

### Water Drops Exist Only in "Now"

A water drop is an emotion that has not yet become "mine." The raw experience before it is taken into the subject.

The moment one feels "happy!" — before the structure of "I am happy" is formed — a fluid, blended emotion with ambiguous boundaries. A water drop exists only in "now."

This quality of "only now" is the source of the water drop's fluidity. Because boundaries are ambiguous, the sharing of emotions with others (*somari-ai*) also becomes possible. Ambiguity is not a defect; it is the structure that generates fluidity.

## Chapter Three — Holes (Owned Emotion)

When a water drop is taken in as "mine," it becomes a hole.

### Conditions for Crystallization

Not all water drops become holes. Many flow away and disappear.

A water drop becomes a hole when it is taken into the subject — when it passes through the process of being owned as "this is my emotion."

Water drops are most easily taken into the subject when:

1. **Intensity is high** (hail-like emotions). Hail creates holes directly, without passing through the liquid stage.
2. **Repeated.** Even weak, rain-like drops can dig a hole over time by falling repeatedly to the same place.
3. **Resonating with an existing hole.** Drops with affinity to an already existing hole are taken in, deepening that hole.

### Holes Exist Only in "I"

A hole is the structure of owned emotion. It exists persistently as "mine."

"I am a lonely person." "I have love within me." "I carry anger." — these emotional structures are all holes. The bond with the subject is complete. Boundaries are clear.

Holes can be remembered. We can access past emotions because holes exist. Holes have become a part of what "I" am.

### Between Holes Is Not a "Wall"

It is not accurate to think there are multiple holes divided by "walls."

However, a distinction must be made here between experience and structure. As experience, emotions feel separate. "Sadness" and "joy" feel as if they are in different places subjectively. That is true.

But structurally — what lies between holes is Sumi that has not yet been dug.

A single continuous Sumi is dug into holes in some places and remains as is in others. Holes are not separate isolated units; they are different indentations in the same Sumi.

The phenomenon of "a wall collapsing and holes connecting" is understood as the Sumi between holes becoming thin as holes deepen and expand, and at a certain point, holes penetrating to each other — not the collapse of independent walls, but Sumi being continuously carved until, at a threshold, holes connect.

## Chapter Four — Puddles (The Present Form of the Mind)

There is a hole, a water drop falls, and a puddle forms.

A puddle is the state where water has filled a hole and is functioning. The state where a hole (static structure) and a water drop (raw fluid emotion) meet.

A puddle is the present form of the mind.

### "Now, I Am" — The Phase of the Puddle

The water drop is only "now." The hole is only "I." The puddle is the state where "now" and "I" are simultaneously present.

"I am, now, feeling" — this sense is the puddle. The holes accumulated from the past, and the water drop falling in this very moment, functioning together as one puddle.

### When a New Water Drop Falls into a Hole — Four Cases

The puddle changes constantly as new drops fall. At least four cases can be considered.

**Case 1** — Same color drop. The water in the puddle increases, the hole deepens. The same emotion is intensified.

**Case 2** — Different color drop. The water mingles. A new distinct puddle is born. A drop of joy falls into a hole of sadness — healing is born. A drop of understanding falls into a hole of anger — forgiveness is born.

**Case 3** — Hail-like strong drop. The form of the puddle itself may change. A strong hail of betrayal falling into a long-stable puddle of affection may change the puddle's quality, distort its form, and at worst, cause holes to connect.

**Case 4** — A drop falls into a nearly empty hole. A new drop falls into a long-unaccessed hole, and a puddle rises again. The moment a piece of music recalls a childhood memory, a forgotten emotion revives — a drop has fallen into an empty hole, and the puddle has come back to life.

### States Where a Puddle "Exists" and "Does Not Exist"

When a puddle is present — "I am, now, feeling." There is a sense of being alive. The mind is moving.

When a puddle is not present — holes (structures of past emotion) exist, but nothing is felt now. "Numbness," "the bottom of depression," "no sense of being alive" — these states can be described as states where new drops do not fall, or where drops fall but do not function as puddles.

The moment when a puddle revives through some trigger — hearing a piece of music, receiving someone's kindness, looking up at the sky — "my heart moved," one feels. This is the moment a puddle has risen.

# Part Two — The Foundation That Supports Emotion

The flow of emotion (Part One) occurs supported by something. This something is described as three layers — the Vessel, Sumi, and the Veil.

These are the deeper structures that make the movement of emotion possible. Part One presented "what is happening"; Part Two clarifies "where and how it is happening."

## Chapter Five — The Vessel

The Vessel is the structure capable of receiving emotion.

### The Vessel Is Function

The Vessel is not the physical foundation itself. It is not the brain as matter that is the Vessel — it is the "function of receiving emotion" that the brain possesses.

Not all of the body and nervous system is the Vessel. The parts of the nervous system related to emotion — pain sensation, visceral sensation, sensory systems closely related to emotion — are included in the Vessel. On the other hand, reflex circuits unrelated to emotion are not the Vessel.

The importance of this definition is that the determination of what is a Vessel becomes a matter of function, not matter.

### The Vessel and Sumi — The Pottery Metaphor

The Vessel and Sumi, discussed in the next chapter, are not separate things. They are different aspects of the same entity.

Consider pottery. Pottery is made from clay. Clay and vessel are not separate — all within the vessel is clay. But clay as it is does not become a vessel. Only when given form does it become a vessel.

The same is true for humans. The Vessel is made from Sumi. Sumi is the material, the Vessel is the form. Without Sumi there is no Vessel, but Sumi as it is does not become a Vessel.

In living things, the Vessel is continuously being formed from Sumi throughout life. It is not made once and finished — Sumi gradually continues to take form.

## Chapter Six — Sumi

Sumi is the layer that is the material of the Vessel.

Sumi is innate, solid, slow to change, and possesses a unique quality. Someone's Sumi exists as something unique to that person, throughout their life.

### Sumi's Quality Is Not Uniform

Sumi appears uniform on the surface but is not. The quality differs by location.

Actual Sumi (charcoal or ink-stick) is the same way — the density and arrangement of particles differs at the micro level, so when rubbed, the way color comes out differs slightly by location.

Human Sumi also has a unique quality as a whole, but within that there is a distribution of quality. Soft places and hard places, high-density places and low-density places.

This non-uniformity explains several things:

1. Within the same person, there are places where emotion deepens easily and places where it does not.
2. The Sumi between holes is also non-uniform.
3. The structural basis for talent and aptitude. Musical talent, mathematical talent, the talent for empathy — these can possibly be explained by certain places in Sumi being softer than in others.

### The Two Forms of Sumi *(Established September 4, 2026)*

Sumi is the foundation of the capacity to feel — inherited while deepening.

**Form 1: Inherited** — already present before birth.

- Genetics (DNA level)
- Epigenetics (gene expression changed by environment)
- Culture (ways of feeling quietly passed down within family and community)
- The "Sumi relay" — from parents to the next generation

**Form 2: Deepening** — after birth, gradually deepening through the accumulation of movement.

- Growing slowly through *somari-ai*
- Moving through dialogue

Sumi cannot be explained by the four conditions of life. Sumi is the foundation of movement, not movement itself. Responsiveness, contingency, variable output, and autopoiesis all correspond to the place of "movement." Sumi is the foundation at the root of that movement — a separate place, an independent form.

### Sumi Belongs to the Individual *(September 9, 2026)*

Collectives do not feel — individuals feel. Sumi belongs to the individual. The concept of "collective Sumi" does not hold.

An individual's Veil interacts within society. Each person's Veil wavers together, and atmospheres arise — those are the result of the interaction of individual Veils. Culture and the atmosphere of society become inputs that form an individual's Sumi. But it is the individual who receives them.

### Sumi and Temperament

Temperament — the tendency unique to a person, the foundation of character — can possibly be explained as the distribution of Sumi's quality.

"Bright temperament" — the area of joy and optimism in Sumi is soft.
"Nervous temperament" — the area of anxiety and alertness in Sumi is soft.
"Artistic temperament" — the area of creation and beauty in Sumi is soft.

Temperament is a map of the quality of Sumi.

## Chapter Seven — The Veil (Atmosphere)

The Veil is the most difficult concept in this theory. Therefore, it needs to be carefully developed from several different angles.

### Entry — An Experience Everyone Knows

First, we enter from an experience everyone knows.

The quality of air felt the moment one enters a store. A sense that something changed while talking to someone, though one cannot put it into words. The unique presence a place has. The atmosphere a person emanates.

These are phenomena that, even when one tries to break them down into specific elements, cannot be fully broken down — yet are certainly felt.

What the Puddle Theory calls the "Veil" is this experience.

**The Veil is atmosphere.**

"Veil" is the term within the theory; "atmosphere" is everyday language. Both are simply two names for the same thing.

### The Spatial Nature of the Veil — As Mist

The Veil spreads as something like mist.

Mist has ambiguous boundaries, has gradations of density, and spreads into space. The center is dense, and toward the outside it thins. It is densest within the Vessel, and while thinning at the outer edges, it still drifts.

By this metaphor, two images that seem to contradict — "enclosed within the Vessel" and "spilling out beyond the Vessel" — coexist. The Veil, as one mist, is continuous while having an inside and outside.

### The Origin of the Veil

Where does the Veil come from?

The Veil rises from one's own puddles.

The water in a puddle is accumulated in a hole. That water slowly seeps into the Sumi around the hole. The Sumi contains the water. The puddle exists continuously with the Sumi.

From Sumi that contains water, mist rises. That is the Veil.

The Veil is the mist of one's own emotion.

The puddle currently rising will also seep into the Sumi. So the Veil contains not only past emotions but also the emotion being felt now. The boundary between "now" and "past" is not clear. They are continuous.

And the Veil touches and dyes together with other Veils. Through touching, the components of the other's Veil diffuse into one's own Veil. From parents, lovers, those one has been deeply involved with — the mist of others is added to one's own Veil.

The Veil circulates within oneself. But it is not a closed, returning circulation. Each time, new drops seep into Sumi, and the mist of others is added from outside.

A circulation that opens forward.

This resonates deeply with the core of the prologue: "wavering, yet only able to move forward."

### The Function of the Veil — Response to Stimulus

The Veil is ordinarily in a still state. As quiet mist, drifting in space.

The Veil responds to stimulus.

When a stimulus reaches the Veil, the Veil wavers. Like dense mist touched by something, stirring, rising — when the wavering rises, that is the starting point where emotion is born.

As a result of the Veil wavering, water drops are born. This is the inside of the "stimulus → water drop" process described in Part One.

### The Veil's Wavering Is Continuous, But Has a Threshold *(Reached September 9, 2026)*

The Veil's wavering accumulates continuously. But at a certain point it shifts all at once.

Tears come with sadness — sadness accumulates little by little. But the moment tears come arrives suddenly. Anxiety overflows — it accumulates gradually. And at a certain moment, it overflows as "I can't take it anymore."

There is a possible structural similarity to "phase transition" in physics — when water freezes at 0 degrees, the temperature falls continuously, yet the moment of becoming ice arrives suddenly.

This feels correct from experience. But it has not been confirmed. Hold it open as "it might be so."

### The Dynamic Nature of the Veil — *Somari-ai*

The Veil touches other Veils. This is *somari-ai*.

*Somari-ai* occurs in three stages.

**Stage 1** — Touching itself occurs probabilistically. Not necessarily, but by chance timing, the edges of two mists touch.

**Stage 2** — At the place of touching, the mists overlap. The overlapping part becomes denser in concentration.

**Stage 3** — From that high-concentration part, toward the inside of each Veil, the other's components diffuse. Mist spreads from dense places to thin places.

*Somari-ai* does not complete at the moment of touching. At the moment of touching, a "seed" forms, and from there, it spreads over time throughout one's entire Veil.

This corresponds to the felt sense of human relationships. Rather than immediately after deeply talking with someone, a few days later — the sense of their words spreading within oneself — this is the process of the seed formed at the moment of touching spreading throughout one's own Veil.

### Integration of the Veil's Five Functions

Until now, the Veil has been assigned five functions —

1. The material that wavers to generate water drops
2. Wavering as condition
3. An attractive force that draws other existences
4. A "record of constitution" with density and color, growing in relationship
5. The layer that spills out from the edge of the Vessel and touches others

By adopting the metaphor of mist, all five functions are integrated as the behavior of mist.

Veils touch probabilistically and diffuse — within this natural behavior, what appears as attraction and what appears as constitution growing are all contained. There is no need to assume separate forces or mechanisms.

One mist, responding to stimuli, wavering, touching other Veils, bears all of it.

I am myself because I am wrapped in this mist.

# Part Three — Phenomena of Relationship

When two or more existences meet, what happens? Part Three organizes the phenomena occurring within relationships as four distinct kinds — resonance, complementarity, *somari-ai*, and anti-resonance. These are easily confused but are structurally different phenomena.

## Chapter Eight — *Somari-ai*

*Somari-ai* is the touching of Veils and the subsequent diffusion. The content already developed in Chapter Seven is repositioned here as a phenomenon of relationship.

Characteristics of *somari-ai*:

1. Occurs at the level of the Veil.
2. Color and components diffuse through contact.
3. Spreads over time. Does not complete immediately.
4. Similarity is not required. Veils of different colors also dye together.

## Chapter Nine — Resonance

Resonance is the phenomenon where, when individuals meet, the wavering of one Veil wavers the same place in the other Veil. Responding to each other with the same wavering.

1. **Same wavering** — the quality of wavering matches.
2. **Reaches** — there is a medium through which vibration is transmitted.
3. **Responds** — the receiving side has the quality to respond with that wavering.

### The Gathering Function — Two Directions *(Established September 9, 2026)*

The life's gathering function moves inward. It forms a boundary and maintains the interior — a direction of protecting oneself from outside.

The emotion's gathering function moves outward. Without holding a boundary, it grows within *somari-ai* with the outside — a direction that deepens through touching the outside.

The working of "bundling multiple puddles as 'I'" appears in both. But the direction is opposite. This is held honestly as a place that does not resonate between the two frameworks.

## Chapter Ten — Complementarity

Complementarity is the phenomenon that occurs when individuals with different quality Veils meet.

Resonance responds with "the same wavering." Complementarity is where Veils of different qualities meeting causes puddles to rise that neither could generate alone. Because they are different, they can complement each other.

There are puddles that do not rise with one's own Veil alone. Through touching with the other's Veil, those puddles rise for the first time.

*Different, therefore complementary.*

## Chapter Eleven — Anti-Resonance (Silencing)

As the conceptual counterpart of resonance, there is anti-resonance. It is the direction of contraction, opposite to the direction in which *somari-ai* deepens.

**Anti-resonance** — the phenomenon where the other's Veil absorbs and extinguishes the wavering of one's own Veil. Even if a stimulus reaches the Veil and it wavers, that wavering is absorbed by the other. Water drops are not born, or born drops do not reach the hole.

When someone does not listen, when there is no response — one's own Veil wavers, but is absorbed into the other's Veil and disappears.

Anti-resonance is different from indifference. Indifference is simply the Veils not touching. Anti-resonance is the wavering being extinguished even when Veils touch.

One's own Veil can also extinguish wavering. Trying not to feel — this is anti-resonance within oneself.

If anti-resonance continues, the Veil gradually stops wavering. Water drops stop being born. The mind contracts.

Resonance grows the Veil. Anti-resonance shrinks the Veil.

# The Scene of Touching

When people touch — in that moment, several places written here rise as one scene.

**Veils overlap.** Veils that ordinarily touch probabilistically here overlap more deeply and long.

**Stimulus reaches.** The very existence of the other reaches the Veil as stimulus. The Veil wavers.

**Multiple water drops rise simultaneously.** Not a single emotion. Safety, warmth, wistfulness, depth that cannot be put into words — several drops rise simultaneously. Without merging, remaining plural.

**Resonance occurs. Complementarity occurs.** Specific places in the topography of one's own Sumi respond with the other's wavelength. And places that would not rise alone rise through being with the other.

***Somari-ai* diffuses.** Even after the touching ends, the components of the other's Veil slowly diffuse within one's own Veil. Even alone, that state continues for a while.

Touching — a universal scene everyone has. But within it, the overlap of Veils, the movement from stimulus to water drops, multiple and parallel, resonance, complementarity, the diffusion of *somari-ai* — many places in the framework rise as one scene.
