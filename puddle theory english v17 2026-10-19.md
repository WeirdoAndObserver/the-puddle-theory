Emotion digs a hole.
A water drop falls there.
And a puddle forms.

That is everything. But within this one line, there are many places. We trace them one by one, carefully.

## The Framework's Stance

Emotion has no form. Therefore the true nature of Sumi cannot be proven through form either. To describe what is unknown as unknown — that honesty is the core of this framework's integrity.

The framework is a map that describes the structure of emotion. The question of what life itself is — that is a separate inquiry. There are places where the structure of emotion and the conditions of life resonate, but resonance is not identity. Not confusing this distinction is another part of the integrity.

---

## Connections to Predecessors

The places this theory has arrived at resonate with the directions earlier thinkers faced.

William James (1842–1910) said consciousness is a stream — not a collection of fixed elements, but something that moves ceaselessly. His observations of crests and resting points — moments that are hard to grasp and fluid, and moments that settle and appear in consciousness — overlap with the movement of water drops and puddles in this framework.

Henri Bergson (1859–1941) said time is a flow that does not return — like a snowball that grows larger each time it rolls, unable to return to the same place, the past does not disappear but lives on, transformed into the present.

Different standing points, same direction: both point to the place where flow is one-directional, and one cannot return to the same place. The framework's "circulation that opens forward" stands on that direction.

The framework was not built by using their theories. In the midst of trying to describe the movement of emotion — we noticed, when we looked up, that we were facing the same direction.

## I. Stimulus — Something Arrives First

> **Essence.** Something always comes first. A sound, a scent, a face, a memory that surfaces — or a silence where a sound was expected. These are all stimuli. Whatever its source, each one travels the same path through you: Veil → water drop → hole → puddle. And underneath that path, deeper than will or feeling, there may be a kind of movement that cannot easily be stopped — out of which will and emotion are gradually formed.

Emotion does not arise suddenly. First, something comes.

**External stimulus** — someone's voice, music, a scent, someone's expression, a landscape that catches the eye. These come from the outside world.

**Energy's absence** — silence, something expected that does not come, the lack of a response from someone. It is not only what is added that counts as stimulus. What is missing — what should have been there but is not — also becomes stimulus.

A silent room, where no sound comes, is not just "nothing." It acts on the one listening, in the form of "no sound."

**Internal stimulus** — a memory that suddenly surfaces, a bodily sensation, an unstoppable thought. These arrive without intention. Obsessive thoughts, flashbacks, cravings — these also rise as internal stimuli, born against one's will, in a place that cannot be stopped.

**Active generation** — trying to produce enjoyment by deciding "let's enjoy this." Trying to produce calm by deciding "let's settle down." Will is an indirect force; it does not last long.

Only weak drops can be made in the direction one wants. But in the direction one does not want, strong drops can rise automatically. "Let's calm down," one thinks, yet anxiety rises on its own — this is the other face of active generation. A sensitized Veil moves against intention.

**Instinct** — appetite, sexual desire, the desire for sleep. Desires already waiting within Sumi, rising from within.

Whatever the source, the stimulus passes through the same process: stimulus → Veil → water drop → hole → puddle. Internal or external, it travels this path. This is why a person may sometimes be unable to distinguish between internal and external stimulus.

One is tempted to rank stimuli by strength — instinct first, internal stimulus second, and so on. But in reality, which is strongest depends on the situation. Hunger can overwhelm everything at times. The sound of hail can blow everything away in an instant (the hail here means a water drop that is intense enough to leave a deep mark in a single fall — in the framework's own words, a different kind of drop from the quiet repetition of rain). Will can hold back instinct for a long time. What is strongest is decided by situation and combination.

### A Movement at the Root That Cannot Easily Be Altered

The movement of stimulus → output is itself there. That movement is difficult to alter — it is a place where "it cannot be otherwise." Simple input-output accumulates and becomes complex. From that complex input-output, will, emotion, and the desire to live may be formed.

"Will and emotion are complexly formed by movements that are difficult to alter" — that place remains open. But the movement is certainly there.

## II. The Veil — Where Stimulus First Arrives

> **Essence.** Stimulus never becomes emotion in one step. It first touches something invisible around you — the Veil, best imagined as a mist that wraps the self. Only when this mist wavers does feeling begin to form. Everyone's Veil is different, and even within one person it is uneven — sensitive in some directions, dull in others. The Veil is not fixed; it changes slowly with each thing that happens to you.

Stimulus does not become emotion directly. First, it arrives at a place called the Veil.

The Veil is an invisible layer that wraps around oneself — think of it as something like mist. The place of first response, before emotion is born.

The Veil functions as the place that receives stimulus. When a stimulus comes and the Veil wavers, emotion begins to arise. Where the Veil does not waver, emotion is not born.

The Veil is, in its usual state, held quiet. But its quietness is not from nothingness. It is held quiet. Emotion rises as a difference from this quietness.

Being held quiet means being in a state where water drops do not easily form. This working is, always, directed toward holding back.

The Veil is held quiet in order to keep the mind's calm. If one were to respond to every stimulus, the mind would wear out.

The Veil's wavering is a difference from the quietness. The difference is not limited to one direction. A stimulus that adds energy, and a stimulus that lacks energy (such as silence, or the absence of a response) — both create a difference from the quietness. In either direction, the difference becomes the beginning of emotion.

The quality of this Veil differs from person to person. People who feel easily, people who feel with difficulty, people who are sensitive only to certain things — those differences come from differences in the Veil. Even within the same person, the Veil is not uniform: sensitive in some directions, dull in others. There is unevenness.

The Veil is formed from Sumi. From Sumi that contains water, mist rises. The quality of Sumi determines the root of the Veil.

The Veil is dynamic — not fixed. It changes little by little as experience accumulates.

## III. From Fragments to Drops — Mist and Leaves

> **Essence.** Inside the Veil, something is always drifting — tiny fragments of feeling, like a fine mist that has no direction of its own. When a stimulus comes, it is like a leaf appearing in that mist: the fragments find a place to pool and gather into a single drop. That drop is the moment emotion takes shape. Why one particular moment lets the drop fall when another did not — that we still do not know.

The Veil is where fragments of emotion flow. Like mist, they drift without direction, endlessly.

Mist drifts continuously — tiny fragments of water, without direction. A leaf receives them — a place for gathering appears. They collect and become a drop — taking form. The drop falls into the puddle — gaining direction, beginning to move.

The size of water drops varies with the Veil's amplitude of wavering. There are also small water drops like drizzle.

When a stimulus reaches the Veil and causes it to waver, fragments gather in one place and become a water drop. That is the moment emotion takes form.

Why does that particular stimulus generate a drop at that particular moment — the trigger remains unknown. But the mechanism of the movement is here. Hold it open as "it might be so."

## IV. Water Drops — The Moment Emotion Is Born

> **Essence.** When the Veil wavers, something comes into focus: a water drop. This is the moment an emotion takes form — the instant you first recognize it as "happy" or "sad" or "angry" or "wistful." A water drop belongs only to right now; yesterday's sadness is yesterday's drop. And from a single stimulus, several drops of different colors can appear at once. "Happy yet wistful" is not a contradiction — it is simply two drops being born together.

When the Veil wavers, a water drop is born.

Water drops have size. There are small water drops like drizzle, and there are ordinary water drops. The difference in size is the difference in the Veil's amplitude of wavering.

A water drop is the moment emotion takes form. "Happy," "sad," "anxious," "angry" — the moment it rises as one cluster.

A water drop belongs only to "now." Yesterday's sadness is not today's water drop. But if yesterday's sadness dug a hole, then even a small stimulus today can easily generate a water drop.

From a single stimulus, multiple water drops can be born simultaneously. "Happy yet wistful," "angry yet sad" — this is not contradiction. Different colored drops are simply born at the same time.

## V. Holes — Where Experience Accumulates and Remains

> **Essence.** Water drops fall into holes. A hole is a place that has been dug, over time, by the same kind of feeling arriving again and again. The deeper the hole, the more easily a new stimulus brings a puddle back. Everyone carries countless holes — holes of grief, of joy, of longing, of anger. Each hole belongs only to "I." That may be where individuality actually lives: not in what we are told we are, but in the shape of the hollows inside us.

Water drops fall into holes.

A hole is a place dug by the repetition of the same emotion. Into a deep hole, even a small stimulus can cause a puddle to rise.

Just hearing the voice of a loved one is enough to move the heart. That is because the experiences with that person have been digging the hole deeper.

There is not one hole — there are holes of sadness, joy, anger, longing. Within a person, there are countless holes.

Having deep holes is not weakness. It is proof of having felt deeply.

Holes belong only to "I." Even with the same music, which hole it falls into differs by person. That may be the true nature of individuality.

## VI. Puddles — The State of "Feeling" Emotion

> **Essence.** When water drops gather in a hole, something rises to the surface: a puddle. This is the state of actually feeling — "now, I am sad," "now, I am happy." Several puddles can rise at once, and that is not confusion but richness; feeling never has to resolve into one thing. In time each puddle settles, and its water seeps back into Sumi, where the next cycle begins. This is why one can never return to exactly the same place — the circulation only opens forward.

When water drops accumulate in a hole, a puddle rises.

This is the state of "feeling an emotion." "Now, I am, sad." "Now, I am, happy." When a puddle is up, emotion has become "mine."

Puddles can rise multiple at once. "Happy yet lonely," "angry yet loving" — this is a normal state. Emotion does not need to converge into one. Multiple remaining parallel — that is one form of richness of the mind.

The deeper the hole, the more drops fall into it. The reason sadness is so deep when a long-loved person is lost is that a deep hole had been dug there. The hole of gratitude and the hole of joy had been deepening at the same time — so sadness and gratitude rise together. The richness with which emotion rises may be proof of how deep the *somari-ai* had been.

The puddle eventually settles. And the water seeps into Sumi, becoming the foundation for the next cycle. This is the "circulation that opens forward." One cannot return to the same place.

When a puddle rises too intensely and spills out of the hole — that is the place where emotion overflows as action.

This circulation holds not only the power to turn, but a built-in power to move forward. The rising of a puddle changes Sumi, and the changed Sumi becomes the foundation for the next cycle. Not stoppage, but transformation. Not a return to the same place, but a gradual unfolding into slightly different places — this is the movement of the "circulation that opens forward."

## VII. Sumi — The Foundation of the Capacity to Feel

> **Essence.** Underneath everything in this theory lies Sumi — the root material that makes feeling possible at all. Without it, no wavering would ever be noticed. Sumi has two sides. One is inherited, already there before you were born, carried from parents to the next generation. The other is deepening — the way density gathers, over a life, into the places you have touched most. The total amount of Sumi within one person does not really grow; what shifts is the shape of its distribution. And Sumi belongs only to the individual — it sits deeper than the four conditions of life, at a level those conditions were never designed to reach.

### 1. What Is Sumi?

Sumi is the foundation of the capacity to feel — the root place that receives stimulus from outside.

When stimulus arrives, the Veil wavers. When the Veil wavers, Sumi is there. Without Sumi, the wavering is not perceived.

Sumi is not the place that wavers. It is the root foundation where wavering is perceived.

### 2. The Two Forms of Sumi

Sumi has two forms.

One is **inherited** — already there before birth. Inherited from two parents, as the ease with which emotional holes are dug. From parents to the next generation, Sumi is relayed.

The other is **deepening** — after birth, as movement accumulates, density gradually gathers, little by little, in specific places. Here, density means the degree to which water can be held. The denser a place, the more water it can hold. The total does not grow; the Sumi that is already there becomes concentrated, unevenly.

Through *somari-ai*, Sumi grows. It moves in dialogue. Each time one meets someone and the heart wavers, the distribution of Sumi's density changes a little — not the total, but the arrangement.

Sumi is already there, and movement accumulates there, deepening. That is the form of the foundation of the capacity to feel.

The two forms may not be separate Sumi. The same density distribution, shown at different moments — what is inherited is the distribution that the parents reshaped through their own growth, handed on as the child's starting point. What deepens is how that distribution continues to change after birth. But this relation is still held open.

### 3. Sumi Cannot Be Explained by the Four Conditions of Life

Elsewhere (in the Life Chapter), the four conditions of life have been traced: responsiveness, contingency, variable output, autopoiesis. Their meanings are given in the Life Chapter (https://github.com/WeirdoAndObserver/life-and-beyond). Here, only responsiveness is noted in one line — a response to stimulus that has a direction, either to change oneself or to maintain oneself. These four correspond to the movement of emotion. They are the conditions for determining the movement of water drops being born, puddles rising, *somari-ai* occurring.

But Sumi cannot be explained by these four conditions.

Responsiveness — Sumi is not the place that responds, but the foundation on which response takes place. Contingency — Sumi is the foundation of the way of wavering; Sumi itself does not waver. Variable output — Sumi is not the output, but the foundation of output. Autopoiesis — Sumi is the foundation on which self-production rests, not the subject that produces.

All are premise of movement, not movement itself. Sumi is at the root of movement — at a different level from what is subject to determination.

Sumi is the unique root place of the Puddle Theory.

### 4. What Is Innate Is the Quality of Sumi (and Instinct)

What is held from birth is the quality of Sumi — the tendency of density distribution — and the instincts contained within Sumi. There are not yet any holes. There is no memory of emotion. The Veil's starting tendencies are determined by the quality of Sumi, but it grows through experience.

Holes are dug by experience. The Veil grows through *somari-ai*. The gathering function of emotion is formed afterward, through accumulation.

Even what is felt as "innate personality" is, for the most part, the quality of Sumi meeting experience and taking shape. The starting point differs, but from there, experience makes the form.

### 5. A Single Line Beginning from Sumi

Because Sumi exists, my emotion grows. Emotion is formed as "mine." There are exceptions, but the basic direction moves this way.

As emotion grows, the gathering function of emotion comes to work. When multiple puddles are rising simultaneously, the function that bundles them as "now, I am feeling."

Through this gathering function, the mind grows.

Sumi → my emotion grows — its forming → the gathering function of emotion → the growth of the mind.

### 6. Places Held Open

There are places about Sumi that remain open.

One is the **origin of Sumi**. Sumi is relayed. Received from parents. Parents received it from their parents. Where did it first come from — this is unknown. "What is, is — we do not consider before that." Hold it open.

Second, the mechanism of the **connecting movement between Sumi and the forming of emotion**. Because the foundation exists, emotion gathers. But how exactly it gathers — the specific mechanism of that movement — is not yet known.

Placing what is unknown as unknown while still thinking — that is the posture of the Puddle Theory.

Sumi lies at the root of the Veil.

Think of it as the person's root, resting quietly at the bottom of a deep sea — the ease and difficulty of feeling held from birth, the directions in which one feels most readily.

Sumi is a receptor. It exists as the foundation that receives stimulus from outside.

The quality of Sumi is the distribution of density. Places that can hold water are soft and easily dug deeper. Places that cannot hold water are hard and dug with difficulty.

The density distribution at birth is the person's initial tendency — the starting point of individuality. From there, with growth and the accumulation of experience, the distribution changes. But it changes slowly. The total amount of Sumi does not change greatly. What changes is which places are dense and which are sparse. This unevenness becomes clearer with growth.

**Sumi Belongs to the Individual**

Collectives do not feel — individuals feel.

Sumi belongs to the individual. The concept of "collective Sumi" does not hold.

But individual Veils interact within society. Each person's Veil wavers together with others, and atmospheres and feelings arise. The air of "we must fight" during wartime. The closed feeling of the Great Depression. No one decided it, yet it is there — the result of the interaction of individual Veils.

Culture and the atmosphere of society become inputs that form an individual's Sumi. But it is the individual who receives them. It is the individual who is dyed.

Even when the atmosphere of society leans in a certain direction, there are people whose Veils do not become dyed. Such a Veil can sometimes correct the movement of a collective that has tilted in the wrong direction. Not being dyed is different from indifference — it means that from a deep root, there is a place that does not waver. Hold it open.

**The Depth and Shading of Sumi**

Sumi exists as unrubbed solid, not as uniform liquid. Rubbed on an inkstone, Sumi becomes a uniform fluid. But the Sumi of this framework is not rubbed — it keeps its gradations and density, as a solid mass.

Places that respond easily to stimulus and places that respond with difficulty remain just as they are. This may be the root of each person's different way of feeling.

Holes have no walls. Because they are made within Sumi, holes neighbor each other through Sumi itself — the Sumi between them seeps and influences across.

Why does repetition deepen a hole? For the same reason water drops wear away rock, or a well-worn shoe heel wears unevenly. The same kind of feeling falls in the same place, again and again, and that place is soaked densely. Repetition produces density — a plain fact of nature and of daily life.

Not every water drop becomes a hole. A water drop whose single strength does not reach the threshold is absorbed into Sumi. This we call noise. Noise, too, seeps into Sumi, and softens it little by little. But noise that keeps falling on the same place — though it may not dig a hole on any single fall — can cross the threshold through accumulation. What digs a hole is a drop that exceeded the threshold — or the movement that lies at the end of accumulated noise.

When the scent of osmanthus arrives and, for no clear reason, longing comes — the stimulus of scent moves the Sumi around which autumn memories and old emotions have soaked, and feeling seeps out slowly. This may be what depth is.

This is different from the moment of joy at passing an exam. That moment is emotion switching on, now. The longing by the osmanthus is the past's seeping, drawn out by the present stimulus — the moment Sumi's depth moves.

Sumi's depth is not determined by length of time alone. Density makes depth. Even someone young, if they have traced deep places, has depth. Age and depth do not necessarily match.

Hold it open as "it might be so."

**The Shifting Density of Sumi, and Aging**

Aging can be drawn as a shifting of density within Sumi. Not shrinking, but density becoming uneven — it can be seen as that movement. The total amount of Sumi, within one person, does not change greatly.

In places where a feeling has been touched deeply and repeatedly, Sumi comes to hold more water — the more the repetition accumulates, the more that place becomes a vessel that can receive more water. For density to rise is not for it to become hard, but for the room to absorb to widen. So the denser a place, the more easily a falling drop digs it. In turn, other places become relatively sparse.

Because holes have no walls, density spreads as a continuous gradient — not sharply cut, but moving gently from dense regions to sparse ones.

So two things happen at once: deeply important feelings come to be felt more strongly, while trivial daily stimuli no longer waver one as they once did.

This unevenness can happen in anyone — aging can be drawn as the extreme form, over long time, of the way density becomes uneven through the accumulation of experience.

Hold it open as "it might be so."

**The Sumi Relay**

Biological inheritance (DNA, epigenetics) and the Sumi relay are different things. Genes and epigenetics are not Sumi itself, but one path along which Sumi is passed.

A child is born from the place where the parents' Veils overlap. There, the Sumi of each — of the sperm and the egg — fuses, and an overlap of Sumi also forms. The sensitivities of both parents mingle, and a new single Sumi is born — this is the Sumi relay.

What is handed on is not the Sumi the parents had at their own birth. It is the Sumi at that moment, after growth and experience have reshaped the density distribution. The child is born with that as its starting point, and continues to reshape it with growth.

When two Sumi meet, they do not mix evenly.

Imagine kneading red and blue clay in the palm of the hand. The pressure, the time, where one begins — each time, the pattern changes. There are blocks that remain fully red, and places where the two dissolve into violet. What results is not a uniform violet, but a single mass carrying its own unevenness.

The Sumi relay is close to this. There are places where one color stays dense, and places where the boundary dissolves into neither. That unevenness itself becomes the new single Sumi.

This is a figure, not a mechanism — not a description of the actual process. What it is meant to carry is one thing: the Sumi relay is not a blending, but an encounter.

The Sumi relay does not occur only between parent and child. It may also move through culture, language, and dialogue.

**The Scattering of Sumi**

At death, the movement of deepening stops — the movement by which density gathered from outside toward inside comes to an end. After that, from dense toward sparse, Sumi begins to scatter. What scatters is Sumi.

The hole on the receiving side remains. "Even when they are gone, the hole remains" — even after Sumi has scattered, the traces of *somari-ai* keep moving within other vessels.

## VIII. The Movement of the Veil — How Emotion Changes

> **Essence.** The Veil never stays still. Each stimulus leaves its mark. The Veil can grow more sensitive in a direction (sensitization), or more numb to a repeated input (habituation), or harder to waver after long sustained wavering. There is also a quiet side of the Veil that keeps instinct from rising uncontrolled. The way the Veil waves is continuous — a gradual accumulation — but it has thresholds, moments when everything shifts at once, like water becoming ice at zero degrees. And sometimes the Veil goes dry (the sand state). Under anti-resonance, it can also become harder to waver — a movement that can be described as protecting oneself.

The Veil does not stand still. Each time it receives stimulus, it wavers and changes little by little.

**Sensitization** — when stimulus comes repeatedly in the same direction, the Veil becomes easier to waver in that direction. It begins to react more strongly to the next stimulus. This is the root of vicious cycles.

**Habituation** — conversely, when the same stimulus continues, one gradually grows accustomed. Stronger stimulus becomes necessary to obtain the same feeling. This is the root of tolerance in addiction.

**Veil exhaustion** — when intense sensitization continues for a long time, the Veil may become harder to waver. This, too, is one of the movements by which the Veil changes, alongside sensitization and habituation.

**The movement that keeps internal stimulus quiet** — the Veil has a place that keeps instinct from rising carelessly. Appetite and sexual desire are always waiting within Sumi, held in check by the quieting place. When this quieting place breaks down, internal stimulus is born without control.

**The Weight of the Veil — Whole and Local**

The Veil has weight. A heavy Veil is hard to waver — stimulus does not pass through easily. A light Veil wavers easily — it receives stimulus strongly. A person whose Veil as a whole is light (receives stimulus strongly), and one whose Veil is heavy (receives with difficulty) — this is an innate difference.

Further, within the same person, the Veil's weight is not uniform. One direction may be extremely light — wavering intensely at even an ordinary stimulus. That is sensory hypersensitivity. Another direction may be extremely heavy — barely wavering at even a strong stimulus. That is sensory hyposensitivity. Hypersensitivity and hyposensitivity can coexist in the same person.

This unevenness of weight is separate from sensitization (a post-natal change) — it is the innate starting tendency of the Veil.

**The Veil's Wavering Is Continuous, but Has a Threshold**

The Veil's wavering accumulates little by little — it is continuous. But at a certain point, it shifts all at once.

Tears come with sadness — sadness accumulates little by little. But the moment tears come arrives suddenly. The moment one feels "I can't stop this anymore" is the place where the threshold is exceeded.

Anxiety overflows — it accumulates gradually. And at a certain moment, it overflows as "I can't take it anymore." That moment of overflowing is the threshold.

In terms of physics, this is the same structure as the phenomenon of water freezing at 0 degrees Celsius. Temperature falls continuously, yet the moment of becoming ice arrives suddenly — what is called "phase transition." The Veil's wavering may have a similar movement.

Where the threshold is determined involves three elements moving simultaneously — the quality of Sumi, the way the Veil has grown (sensitization, habituation, exhaustion), and the depth of holes. The reason why some people cry watching the same film and others do not may be because these three combinations differ.

This feels correct from experience. But it has not been confirmed — hold it open as "it might be so."

**The Fixation of Emotion** — when the same emotion is repeated, the hole becomes deeper and deeper. Into a deep hole, even a small stimulus brings a puddle. And that puddle begins to rise constantly — until other emotions can no longer be seen.

Sensitization makes a vicious cycle. When the Veil is made to waver repeatedly in a particular direction, more water drops are born on the next stimulus. Fixation deepens this way.

Will is an indirect force — it has trouble reaching the depth of a fixed hole. This is why "just try harder" does not work.

But complete fixation does not exist. However deep a hole may be, it is never the last hole. If a single water drop falls into a hole closer to the sky, a puddle rises again. The possibility never becomes zero.

**The Sand State** — a dry state where holes are not dug, or where water does not seep in even when a hole is dug. Water drops come but slide away. A state where emotion has become difficult to move.

In the sand state, trying to enter through emotion or will does not easily reach. Sumi is dry. At such times, just "eat, excrete, sleep, wake." There is no need to think of anything else. That time alone returns water to Sumi.

**The Veil Becoming Harder to Waver** — when anti-resonance continues, the Veil gradually becomes harder to waver. This can also be described as a movement of protecting oneself.

## IX. Somari-ai — Deep Touching with Others

> **Essence.** "Alone, the mind does not grow." *Somari-ai* is what happens when someone else's Veil wavers and that wavering reaches your own — not through words, not through explanation, but simply through shared presence. Each experience of *somari-ai* changes the concentration of your Veil, little by little. And this exchange is not limited to the living: a recording from 1975 can waver your Veil tonight. The written word of someone long gone can still dye the Veil of a reader today. *Somari-ai* moves across time.

"Alone, the mind does not grow."

*Somari-ai* is the movement by which the wavering of another's Veil reaches one's own. It occurs without exchanging words. It occurs simply by being in the same space.

### Changes to the Veil Through Somari-ai

As experiences of *somari-ai* accumulate, the concentration of one's own Veil changes. Experiences with the other shape one's own Veil.

### The Shading of the Veil — One Form of the Movement of Somari-ai

The Veil is not uniform. Some places are heavy, others light. This difference may be the root of how easily the Veil wavers.

Weight, and the concentration described next (the degree to which particles cluster in the Veil), may be the same quantity seen in a different place and at a different time. One's overall level is set from birth. The level at a particular place rises temporarily when touching another Veil, and goes up and down through sensitization and habituation. But because heavy places and light places are mixed together, local increases and decreases cancel each other out, and the overall level may not change greatly. A place where concentration is high is one that wavers easily.

When two Veils meet, a difference in concentration arises at the overlap. The overlapping place becomes more concentrated, and wavers more easily (becomes sensitive to stimulus). The difference in concentration here, seen at the micro level, is the difference between where particles are packed close and where they are scattered. That concentration gradually spreads toward the less concentrated direction. What spreads lingers for a while within each Veil — this may be one form of the movement of *somari-ai*.

Even between two low-concentration Veils, the overlapping place becomes more concentrated. From that more concentrated place, color slowly seeps into both — an image close to the lived feel of ink wash painting.

This is a place already drawn in the figure of June 2026 (two Veils touching). What was compressed into the phrase "overlap (concentration gradient forms)" has now surfaced as explicit movement.

Hold it open as "it might be so." The relation to Sumi's shading — the word "shading" is shared, but whether there is a deeper connection remains unknown.

### Incomplete Somari-ai

The other's Veil is certainly wavering. But the "meaning" of that wavering cannot be read. Therefore water drops are not born in one's own Veil. Emotional resonance does not occur — a state where the score can be seen but the music cannot be heard.

### Anti-resonance

A movement where the other's Veil is wavering, but it disappears without reaching one's own Veil. When anti-resonance continues, the Veil becomes harder to waver.

### Quiet Somari-ai

Favorite music, favorite books, favorite paintings. There, the traces of someone's Veil exist. A quiet entrance with less risk of anti-resonance. Not pushing, not completely withdrawing — that is the form that protects *somari-ai*.

### The Partner in Somari-ai Need Not Be Alive

A performance recorded in 1975 can waver tonight's Veil. Words someone wrote can continue to change the Veils of those who read them. *Somari-ai* transcends time.

### The Feeling of Those Left Behind

Even after losing a beloved person, that person's hole remains within oneself. Into that hole, memories fall as water drops. The memory of their voice falls.

"Even when they are gone, the hole remains" — the hole on the other's side may be gone, but the hole on the receiving side remains. The traces of interaction continue a new generation in a different vessel.

### Connection to Society

Society is not "one large Veil." Society is shaped based on the emotional structures that individual humans hold.

Emotional contagion does not "create new emotion" — it "amplifies what is already there." A stimulus comes to an existing hole, and the puddle wavers greatly. Without a hole, it does not waver.

One person's Veil changing goes on to influence the collective.

## X. The Gathering Function — Bundling Multiple Emotions as "I"

> **Essence.** When several puddles rise at once, something gathers them together into "now, I am feeling." That gathering function is not something you are born with — it grows across your life, through *somari-ai*. In life and in emotion, this gathering function faces opposite directions: life pulls inward to protect a boundary, while emotion opens outward and grows by being touched.

Multiple puddles are rising. There is a function that gathers these as "now, I am feeling."

This function is not present from birth. It grows through the accumulation of experience. Through *somari-ai* the Veil grows, and emotion is refined — that is what the growth of the mind means.

When this function weakens, a sense of emotion becoming fragmented arises. "The sense of not being oneself," "the feeling of not being here" — this can be described as a state in which, even though holes remain, the function of bundling multiple puddles as "I" has weakened.

### The Gathering Function — Two Directions

Life's gathering function moves inward. It forms a boundary and maintains the interior — a direction of protecting oneself from outside.

Emotion's gathering function moves outward. It grows within *somari-ai*, and through touching others "I" is formed — a direction that deepens through touching the outside.

The working of "bundling multiple puddles as 'I'" is the same. But the direction is opposite.

This is held honestly as a place that does not resonate. What lies beyond here — responsiveness, the boundary line with life — is traced in the Life Chapter (https://github.com/WeirdoAndObserver/life-and-beyond).

## XI. The Veil Does Not Depend on Language

> **Essence.** Feeling does not need language. The Veil grows without words. *Somari-ai* happens without words. Holes are dug without words. Elephants return to touch the bones of their dead, year after year, and know their own herd's bones from strangers'. An orca mother in 2018 carried her dead calf for seventeen days across a thousand miles. Octopuses have personalities and favorites — and dislikes. Even in the human brain, the areas for language and the areas for emotion sit in different anatomical places. Even when the language area is damaged, emotion remains.

The Puddle Theory describes a structure that does not depend on language. It is not a matter of time — of coming before language — but of structure: this framework does not require language to stand.

The Veil grows without language. *Somari-ai* occurs without language. Holes are dug without language.

Elephants return to touch the bones of deceased companions even years later. Moreover, they show a special response to the bones of their own herd — clearly different from the bones of unknown elephants. Elephants live in matrilineal herds, multiple generations from grandmother to grandchild living together. That long *somari-ai* digs deep holes. So even years later, a puddle rises before the bones. "Even when they are gone, the hole remains."

In 2018, a killer whale named Tahlequah carried her dead calf for 17 days, over about 1,000 miles. Each time the calf began to sink, she lifted it with her nose, and members of the herd took turns helping. Without language, that depth exists.

Octopuses have individual "personalities." Some individuals spray ink only at disliked caretakers. With a completely different structure from humans — two-thirds of neurons distributed in the arms — the color of the Veil differs. How each one is dyed differs.

The language area and the emotional area are in anatomically separate positions within the brain. Even when the language area is damaged, emotion remains — this is direct evidence that language and emotion are separate circuits.

This framework may not need to be limited to humans. Elephants, orcas, octopuses — wherever a system has a Veil that wavers, this map may reach. But the color of the Veil differs. The same structure may be moving in a completely different form. Hold it open.

## The Boundary Line — Whether the Veil Wavers

> **Essence.** What separates something that feels from something that does not? Not whether it emits stimulus — many things do that. The real line is whether, when stimulus arrives, something inside wavers. A CD emits music, but has no Veil to be wavered. A rock receives stimulus, but has no place in it that can waver. The Puddle Theory is not a theory of consciousness; it is a theory of the capacity to feel — of sentience.

Where is the boundary of whether something "feels"?

It is not whether stimulus is emitted. Whether stimulus is received and the Veil wavers — that is the boundary line.

A CD emits music. But the CD itself has no Veil. It has no place to waver. A rock also receives stimulus, but has no place to waver.

Systems where the Veil wavers — they may possess the capacity to feel. Systems where the Veil does not waver — even if stimulus is received, processing may occur, but feeling is not born.

The Puddle Theory stands not as "a theory of consciousness" but as "a theory of the capacity to feel (sentience)."

Whether animals have a Veil — the examples of elephants, orcas, and octopuses suggest the possibility. Then what of plants, of fungi? There is research suggesting that trees exchange signals through underground networks (interpretations differ among researchers). Looking at structure alone, there appears to be a movement of stimulus arriving, adjustment entering, and response deepening. But between "the structure is the same" and "the capacity to feel is there" lies a great distance. Hold it carefully open.

## What the Framework Can and Cannot Describe

**Can describe** — the structure of how emotion moves. Why emotion becomes fixed. How *somari-ai* changes the mind. Where the boundary line is. How society relates to emotion.

**Cannot describe** — how to treat. How to choose behavior. Here two questions diverge. The mechanism by which one is chosen from a contested probability can be traced in principle (that is "being determined"). But whether there is someone choosing — that question stands in a place that cannot be reached in principle (that is "deciding"). What the true nature of Sumi is. Why emotion exists. And the question of what life itself is — that is a separate inquiry.

"Emotion has no form. Therefore the true nature of Sumi cannot be proven through form either. To describe what is unknown as unknown — that honesty is the core of this framework's integrity."

Emotion digs a hole.
A water drop falls there.
And a puddle forms.

Emotion has two sides: a side that over time shapes behavior (holes and Sumi carry this), and a side of whether something is being felt in this very moment (water drops and puddles carry this). The framework holds both as structure.

The water drop is "now" only.
The hole is "I" only.
The puddle is "now, I am."

Wavering, yet only able to move forward.
